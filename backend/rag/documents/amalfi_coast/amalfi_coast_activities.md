# Amalfi Coast, Italy — Things to Do

The Path of the Gods is the marquee hike of the Amalfi Coast, a roughly 8-kilometre trail running along the cliffside between Bomerano and Nocelle (the upper village above Positano). The walking time is 3–4 hours one way at moderate pace, with sweeping views of the entire coastline, the Galli Islands, and Capri on clear days. The trail is mostly downhill in the Bomerano-to-Nocelle direction, which is the standard hiking direction for this reason. From Nocelle, an additional 1,500 stone steps descend into Positano. The hike requires reasonable fitness, water (no facilities along the route), proper footwear, and an early start in summer; mid-day attempts in July and August border on dangerous.

Beach time is central to the coast's appeal but expectations should be calibrated. The beaches are mostly small, mostly pebble or coarse sand, and almost entirely lined with private beach clubs that charge €25–80 per person for a sunbed and umbrella for the day. Free public beach sections exist but are small and crowded. Marina Grande in Positano is the main beach and the most photographed; Fornillo, accessible by a short walk west, is smaller and quieter. The coast's most beautiful swimming is often from boats anchored in small coves accessible only by sea — chartering a small boat for a half-day or full-day is one of the most rewarding ways to experience the coast.

Ferry hopping between villages is itself a memorable experience. The seasonal ferry network connects Salerno, Amalfi, Positano, Sorrento, and Capri, with most routes running every 1–2 hours between approximately 9 a.m. and 6 p.m. from April through October. Day trips to Capri are available from all the major coastal towns; the 30–60 minute crossing combined with island time produces a long day but the famous Blue Grotto, the chairlift to Monte Solaro, and the cliffside town of Anacapri justify the effort.

Ravello deserves a dedicated half-day. Villa Rufolo, with its cliff-edge gardens, hosts the annual Ravello Festival of classical music in July and August; performances on the famous outdoor stage with the sea as backdrop are among the most striking concert experiences in Italy. Villa Cimbrone's Terrace of Infinity, lined with classical busts and overlooking a near-unbroken view of the coastline, is the single most photographed viewpoint in Ravello. The town itself is small and walkable; arriving by morning bus from Amalfi (45 minutes including the steep switchback climb) and lunching in the village is the natural rhythm.

The town of Amalfi rewards a few hours of exploration. The Cathedral of Sant'Andrea, with its striking striped facade and Cloister of Paradise, dominates the central piazza. The Paper Museum, housed in a 13th-century paper mill in the valley above the town, documents the Amalfi paper-making tradition that supplied much of medieval Europe; entry is inexpensive and the working hydraulic equipment is genuinely interesting. The Valle delle Ferriere, a rare temperate fern habitat with waterfalls, lies further up the same valley and offers a 5-kilometre loop hike from the town centre.

Cooking classes have proliferated as the coast has grown into a culinary destination. The classes typically focus on Campanian regional specialties — fresh pasta from scratch, the characteristic limone-heavy sauces, scialatielli (the local pasta shape), and cassata or sfogliatella for dessert. Quality varies; classes that include a market visit and small group sizes (under eight participants) tend to produce the best experiences. Costs run €100–180 per person.

Lemon farm visits are a near-mandatory experience given the cultivation's centrality to the regional economy. The Sfusato Amalfitano lemon, with IGP protected designation, is grown on terraced hillsides held up by ancient stone walls. Small family farms offer guided tours that cover the cultivation methods, the production of limoncello, and tastings of lemon-based products from gelato to pasta. The tours run 1–2 hours and cost €15–30 per person.

For visitors interested in classical archaeology, the day trips to Pompeii (1–1.5 hours from the coast by train and bus) and Herculaneum (slightly closer) are essential. Pompeii's scale is vast and a full day with a guide is the recommended visit; Herculaneum is smaller, better preserved in many areas, and can be covered in 2–3 hours. Combining either with an ascent of Vesuvius makes for a long but achievable day trip.

Mount Vesuvius itself can be hiked from the parking area at 1,000 metres to the crater rim at 1,281 metres in about 30 minutes each way; the views over the Bay of Naples on clear days are exceptional. Tickets are timed and best booked in advance during peak season.
