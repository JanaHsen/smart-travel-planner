# Patagonia, Argentina — Things to Do

Trekking is Patagonia's defining activity and El Chalten the unofficial trekking capital. The town sits at the trailhead for an extensive network of day hikes within Los Glaciares National Park, all of which begin from the village itself with no transport required. The Laguna de los Tres trail, leading to a glacial lake at the base of Mount Fitz Roy, is the marquee day hike — 22 kilometres round trip, 8–10 hours, and finishing with a brutal final hour of steep scree. Laguna Torre, a slightly easier 18-kilometre trail, leads to a lake facing the Cerro Torre spire. Multiple shorter walks of two to four hours suit visitors with less time or mixed-ability groups. Camping is permitted at designated sites within the park and is free.

The Perito Moreno Glacier, accessed from El Calafate, is the most visited single attraction in southern Patagonia. The standard visit involves a guided walk along boardwalks facing the glacier's 60-metre-high ice wall, where ice calving — house-sized chunks falling into the lake — happens every few hours with thunderous crashes. More active visitors can book mini-trekking or full ice-trekking excursions that involve crampons and walking on the glacier surface itself; mini-trek runs around 3 hours on the ice and is suitable for reasonably fit visitors with no prior experience.

Wildlife viewing on the Valdes Peninsula, on Patagonia's Atlantic coast near Puerto Madryn, is timed strictly by season. Southern right whales gather in the bay between June and December, with peak numbers in September and October; whale-watching boats run multi-hour trips with high success rates during the season. Orcas hunt sea lions on the peninsula's beaches in February and March; this is one of the few places in the world where this behaviour can be reliably observed from shore. Magellanic penguin colonies, particularly at Punta Tombo further south, contain hundreds of thousands of nesting pairs from October through March.

Bariloche and the surrounding Lake District offer a different character of activity. The Circuito Chico, a 60-kilometre scenic loop around the lake, can be driven, cycled, or partially hiked; multiple stops include viewpoints, small breweries, and access to swimming beaches in summer. The Cerro Catedral ski resort is South America's largest, with extensive intermediate and advanced terrain and a season running roughly mid-June through early October. Rafting on the Manso River, multi-day backpacking circuits in Nahuel Huapi National Park, and fly fishing on the local rivers all draw substantial visitor numbers.

Ushuaia and Tierra del Fuego specialize in expedition-style tourism. The Tierra del Fuego National Park, immediately west of the city, offers day hikes, kayaking on Lapataia Bay, and the famous "End of the World Train" — a small steam railway running into the park along an old prison construction route. Multi-day boat trips to nearby penguin colonies and through the Beagle Channel reach destinations otherwise inaccessible by road. Ushuaia is the primary Antarctic gateway, with eight to ten-day expedition cruises departing throughout the southern summer; pricing for these starts around $5,500 and runs well into five figures for premium operators.

Horseback riding on traditional estancias remains a quintessential Patagonian experience. Working ranches across the region open their grounds to visitors for half-day rides, full-day excursions including a traditional asado lunch, or multi-night stays that combine riding with farm work. The estancias around El Calafate and in the steppe between Bariloche and El Bolson are well-established for visitors. Skill level requirements vary; beginner-friendly options are widely available.

Less mainstream activities reward longer visits. Climbing the granite walls and ice routes around El Chalten draws serious alpinists from across the world; the Fitz Roy and Cerro Torre summits are among the most technically demanding climbs on the planet. Birding throughout Patagonia is exceptional — the region hosts Andean condors, several flamingo species, rheas, and a wide range of seabirds. Astronomy benefits from the dark skies and dry air; the area around El Leoncito Observatory, further north, hosts dedicated stargazing tours.

The Carretera Austral on the Chilean side, while strictly outside Argentine Patagonia, is frequently included in extended itineraries — a 1,200-kilometre largely unpaved road connecting remote fjord towns, glaciers, and rainforest. Crossing into Chile from Argentina is straightforward at multiple points but adds logistical complexity to itineraries.
