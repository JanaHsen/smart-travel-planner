# Maldives — Practical Information

Accommodation pricing in the Maldives spans an enormous range and the choice of accommodation type defines the entire trip experience and budget. Local-island guesthouses run $60–200 per night for a double room, typically with breakfast included; this is the budget-friendly option that has emerged since 2009 and offers the most authentic cultural experience. Mid-range resorts on private islands run $400–900 per night per couple all-inclusive, including return seaplane or speedboat transfers, full board meals, and a basic set of activities. High-end resorts run $1,000–3,000 per night per couple all-inclusive. Ultra-luxury properties — the Soneva, Cheval Blanc, Patina, and similar premium names — start around $2,500 and can exceed $10,000 per night for premium overwater villas or beach pavilions in peak season. Honeymoon and anniversary packages with dining experiences, spa treatments, and private excursions add $500–3,000 per couple over the base rates.

The all-inclusive pricing structure at resorts is universal but the inclusion definitions vary widely. Full-board plans typically cover three meals per day at the main restaurant; specialty restaurants, premium beverages, motorized water sports, scuba diving, and excursions are usually charged separately. All-inclusive plans include alcohol and most non-motorized activities but still typically exclude scuba diving, spa treatments, and certain specialty dining. Carefully reading the inclusion details and budgeting for likely add-ons is essential — the actual all-in cost of a trip is frequently 30–60% above the headline accommodation rate.

Transfers from the airport are non-trivial both in cost and time. Speedboat transfers run $80–250 per person for resorts within roughly 60 kilometres of Malé; seaplane transfers, required for more distant atolls, cost $400–800 per person and only operate during daylight hours, frequently requiring an overnight stay in Malé or at the airport hotel for guests with late-arriving international flights. Domestic flights to regional airports plus speedboat transfers are an alternative for the most distant southern atolls. Resorts arrange transfers as standard; the costs are usually built into the package or added as a fixed line item.

Food costs vary enormously by venue. At resort restaurants outside the included meal plans, dinner mains run $40–120; specialty restaurant tasting menus cost $200–450 per person. At local-island guesthouses and the small restaurants on inhabited islands, meals run $8–20 per person for fresh fish curries, Maldivian short eats, and reheis (the local breakfast of tuna, coconut, and chili eaten with flatbread). Imported alcohol at resorts is expensive — wine bottles run $80–250, cocktails $15–25 — reflecting both the import situation and the captive-market resort pricing model.

Connectivity at resorts is generally excellent and free WiFi is universal at all but the most remote properties. On local islands, mobile data via Dhiraagu or Ooredoo SIM cards (purchased at the airport for $15–30) provides reliable coverage in inhabited areas. Resort guests rarely need a local SIM as the in-villa WiFi handles all communications needs. International roaming on most major carriers works at resorts but is expensive without a Maldives-specific package.

Currency on resort islands operates almost entirely in US dollars — bills are presented in dollars and credit cards are universal. The Maldivian rufiyaa is the national currency but is rarely needed by resort visitors. On local islands and in Malé, rufiyaa is the standard currency for everyday transactions; ATMs are available in Malé and on the larger inhabited islands. Credit card acceptance at local guesthouses is now common but cash dollars or rufiyaa is the safer default for small purchases. Tipping is appreciated but not aggressively expected; $5–10 per day for housekeeping, $20–50 for excellent dive guides at the end of a stay, and 10% added at resort restaurants are reasonable conventions.

Health considerations for the Maldives are generally minor. Routine vaccinations are sufficient; no malaria risk exists in the country. Sun exposure is the most realistic concern given the equatorial latitude and the strong reflection from white sand and water; reef-safe sunscreen (chemical sunscreens are now banned in some Maldivian waters) is essential and should be applied generously and frequently. Coral cuts can become infected quickly in tropical conditions; antibiotic ointment and waterproof bandages are worth having. Tap water is desalinated and generally potable at resorts but bottled water is the standard at meals and can be drunk freely.

Seaplane operations are weather-dependent and operate only during daylight hours (approximately 6 a.m. to 4 p.m.). Late-arriving international flights frequently mean that seaplane transfer is impossible until the next morning, requiring either an overnight at the airport hotel ($150–250) or at one of the small Hulhumalé hotels nearby. Confirming transfer logistics with the resort before booking flights is essential to avoid frustrating layover arrangements.

The Maldives observes the standard Islamic week with Friday and Saturday as the weekend; government offices and many local-island businesses close on Fridays. Resort operations are unaffected by local weekly patterns. Ramadan (dates shift annually) brings significant changes to local-island life — daytime restaurants close on inhabited islands — but resort operations continue normally. The country observes UTC+5 with no daylight saving adjustment.

Visa requirements for most nationalities are straightforward — a free 30-day tourist visa is issued on arrival to nearly all visitors, with proof of onward travel and confirmed accommodation required. Extensions are available through the immigration office in Malé. Currency import limits, drug laws (extremely strict), and prohibitions on importing pornography, religious materials of non-Islamic faiths in quantity, and pork products apply at customs; resort visitors rarely encounter issues but should not bring prohibited items.
