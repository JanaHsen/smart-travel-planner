# Barcelona, Spain — Practical Information

Accommodation pricing in Barcelona has risen substantially over the past decade. Hostel dorm beds run €25–45 per night; private rooms in hostels and budget guesthouses €70–120. Mid-range hotels in central neighbourhoods cost €120–250 per night for a double. Boutique hotels in Eixample and El Born run €250–500. The high-end hotel tier — Hotel Arts, Mandarin Oriental, El Palace, Casa Fuster — starts around €400 and runs to €2,000+ per night for premium suites. Short-term rental regulation has tightened significantly; legitimate licensed apartment rentals are still widely available but unlicensed listings are increasingly restricted and visitors should verify license status before booking. Peak summer (June–August) and the major business conference periods (Mobile World Congress in late February/early March, primary trade shows in October) see prices roughly 40–80% above shoulder rates.

Food costs are moderate. A casual lunch — sandwich, pastry, coffee — runs €8–15 per person. The menú del día, the standard weekday lunch deal of two courses with bread and a drink, costs €12–20 at neighbourhood restaurants and represents the best food value in the city. Dinner at a casual restaurant runs €25–45 per person; mid-range restaurants charge €40–70 per person for two courses with wine. Tapas culture rewards multiple small plates across several venues; budget €30–50 per person for a tapas crawl with drinks. Coffee at a stand-up bar costs €1.50–2.20; the same coffee at an outdoor table on a popular square costs €3–5. Tipping is not expected in the way it is in North America; rounding up the bill or leaving 5–10% for excellent service is the local convention.

Public transport is excellent and the standard mode for visitors. The metro network covers the entire city efficiently, with single tickets at €2.55 and the T-Casual 10-trip ticket at €12.55 representing significantly better value for visits longer than a few days. The Hola BCN tourist pass offers unlimited transit for 2–5 days at €17–40 and includes airport rail service. The bus and tram networks complement the metro for areas not directly served. Bicycles via the Bicing public bike share are available to residents but require local registration; private bike rental shops offer day rentals at €15–25. Walking is the most rewarding way to explore the central districts; the historic core can be crossed entirely on foot in 30–40 minutes.

The airport (BCN), 12 kilometres southwest of the city, connects to the centre via three options. The metro Line 9 Sud connects to both terminals at €5.50 (single ticket, 30–40 minutes to central transfer points). The R2 Nord Renfe rail line runs every 30 minutes at €4.60 (20 minutes to Passeig de Gràcia). The Aerobús express bus runs every 5–10 minutes at €7.25 (35 minutes to Plaça Catalunya) and is the most luggage-friendly option. Taxis from the airport cost €30–40 to the city centre. Renfe high-speed trains from Madrid (2.5–3 hours, €40–120) and Paris (6.5 hours, €70–200) are alternatives to flying for visitors arriving from elsewhere in Europe.

Cards are universally accepted at hotels, restaurants, museums, and most shops. Visa, Mastercard, and increasingly Apple Pay and Google Pay are standard; American Express is accepted at upscale establishments. Smaller bars, traditional bodegas, and certain market stalls remain cash-preferred or cash-only. ATMs are plentiful but charge a €5–8 fee per withdrawal at non-Spanish-bank machines; using bank-branded ATMs of the major Spanish banks (CaixaBank, BBVA, Santander) at their own branches avoids the fee. The euro is the currency.

Connectivity is excellent. EU roaming agreements mean European Union visitors use their domestic plans without surcharge. Visitors from outside the EU can buy tourist SIMs from Vodafone, Movistar, Orange, or the budget operator Yoigo at the airport or in city electronics shops; multi-week packages with substantial data run €15–30. Free WiFi is universal at cafes, restaurants, hotels, and metro stations.

Safety considerations centre primarily on petty theft, which is genuinely a problem in Barcelona at a level that catches some visitors off guard. Pickpocketing on Las Ramblas, in the metro, and at the major tourist attractions is highly organized and persistent; standard precautions (no valuables in back pockets, no phones on outdoor restaurant tables, no large bills on display) substantially reduce risk but do not eliminate it. Violent crime against tourists is uncommon. Fake-petition scams, distraction techniques, and the "found ring" scam operate around the major attractions — politely declining unsolicited approaches is generally sufficient.

The Catalan and Spanish languages have specific patterns worth knowing. Greetings (bon dia in Catalan, buenos días in Spanish), thanks (gràcies / gracias), and please (si us plau / por favor) are appreciated in either language; locals will switch to whichever the visitor uses. English is widely spoken in tourist-area businesses but considerably less so in non-tourist neighbourhoods; basic phrases meaningfully improve interactions in those settings.

Schedule conventions differ from North American norms in ways that catch some visitors. Lunch is generally eaten between 1:30 and 4 p.m.; dinner before 9 p.m. is unusual and many restaurants do not serve dinner before 8:30. Many small shops close from 1:30 to 4:30 p.m. and reopen until 8 or 9 p.m. Department stores and large chains operate continuously. Sunday closures of small shops and many restaurants are common; planning around them is essential. The major cultural attractions operate on tighter and varying schedules — many museums close on Mondays, the Sagrada Família's hours shift seasonally, and last entry to most attractions is 30–60 minutes before closing.

Visa requirements for most Western nationalities are simple — Schengen Area entry for stays up to 90 days within any 180-day period for visa-exempt travellers. Spain enforces the standard Schengen rules. ETIAS authorization will become required for visa-exempt visitors during the rolling 2025–2026 implementation; checking current requirements before booking is the practical approach.
